Scans
-----
One bond, angle or dihedral can be swept rigidly or with constrained relaxation.
Controls include atom picking/indices, both endpoints, point count, maximum
steps per point, extra fixed coordinates, fragment movement and stop on failure.
Dummy atoms are removed with correct remapping of real-atom indices; references
to dummy atoms are rejected before running.

ORCA supports its built-in driver or Beavyr's optimizer. Behemoth supports its
native driver or Beavyr's optimizer. Other backends use Beavyr's driver.
The Behemoth CLI/input was extended with max_cycles, move_fragment,
stop_on_failure, constraints, bond_motion and coordinates. Native Behemoth
scans use its existing HF/DFT/MP2/RI-MP2 gradient-capable frontend.

Rigid bond scans can move the first atom, second atom, or both symmetrically.
When fragment movement is enabled, the corresponding attached groups move.
Relaxed Beavyr/Behemoth scans can select primitive or Pulay redundant internal
coordinates. ORCA's native relaxed scan retains its own optimization controls.

Results automatically open independent Scan Results and Scan Plot windows.
The plot can be reopened, and hovering a point shows its coordinate and energy.
Relative energies default to kcal/mol and can be changed to kJ/mol. The first
or last displayed point can be the reference. Reverse order affects the plot,
numerical table, clipboard CSV, saved CSV/XYZ and loaded trajectory together.
The original calculated absolute energies and geometries remain unchanged.
Load as Trajectory keeps its original label, loads the displayed point order,
opens the Trajectory panel, and starts stopped in Once mode.
Saved scan.xyz/scan.csv/scan_results.ron in the run directory retain original
calculation order. Failed/nonconverged points and partial results are reported.

MECP
----
State A and State B multiplicities are independently specified, with a common
charge, HF/DFT method, basis and geometry. Both states are validated against
the electron count. Identical multiplicities are rejected. Supported backends
are ORCA, Behemoth, PySCF and Psi4. DREIDING and other unverified spin-surface
models are excluded from this workflow.

ORCA offers ORCA built-in or Beavyr MECP. The native input uses Opt and
%mecp SurfCrossOpt true / Mult, with State A in the xyz coordinate block.
The exact user-selected executable is invoked with its input filename.
No MPI discovery, environment setup, executable substitution or fixed user
executable path was added to production code.
Native success requires normal termination, explicit optimization convergence,
and an acceptable final absolute state-energy gap. The final gap limit is a
post-run check; ORCA uses its own optimization convergence criteria.

The Beavyr MECP driver uses Cartesian BFGS by default, with primitive internal
and Pulay redundant internal alternatives. It saves both surface energies,
their difference and the geometries, supports cancellation, and rechecks
convergence at the actual returned geometry. The summary provides Copy MECP
results and saved CSV/XYZ/text files. Trajectory energies are the mean of the
two state energies. A crossing optimization does not perform the adapted
frequency calculation needed to establish curvature along the crossing seam.

TS and minimum searches
----------------------
The existing Behemoth-derived optimizer algorithms were reused, not duplicated.
The TS algorithm matches Behemoth apart from the local linear-algebra adapter;
its only source changes here add serialization for calculator settings.
New named geometry_search modules provide settings, validation, controls,
backend connections, minimum dispatch, native Behemoth parsing and tests.

ORCA TS: native OptTS or Beavyr. Native Calc_Hess is enabled initially;
Recalc_Hess uses the selected interval, with zero omitting periodic rebuilds.
Beavyr TS: Cartesian, primitive internal and Pulay redundant internal modes;
exact/model initial Hessian where supported; internal/Cartesian tracking;
bond, angle, dihedral, transfer, weighted and Cartesian-direction references;
additional connectivity, trust controls and adaptive Hessian settings.
Defaults match Behemoth CLI: primitive internal, exact initial Hessian,
internal tracking, 100 cycles, periodic recalculation disabled, Eckart projection.
Only a converged structure with a calculated final Hessian and one negative
mode is accepted as a verified local TS. A stationary minimum is rejected.

PySCF and xTB TS use Beavyr. Native Psi4 is retained with Beavyr as an option.
Behemoth TS invokes its own --opt-ts driver (currently GFN1-xTB only).
Its CLI and @opt input now expose ts_max_cycles / --ts-max-cycles, and stdout
reports the final negative-mode count. All its existing TS CLI controls are
passed explicitly; no personal executable paths are embedded in application
code. Two new CLI/input tests validate the step limit and reject zero.

Minimum searches expose native/Beavyr choices for ORCA, PySCF, Psi4 and xTB.
Behemoth retains its own native minimum optimizer. The local default is
primitive internal BFGS; Pulay and Cartesian choices use the existing modules.
BFGS and steepest descent are available in internal coordinates; Cartesian
also supports the two Barzilai-Borwein/two-point gradient choices.
PySCF local optimization uses energy/gradient evaluations and does not import
geomeTRIC. Follow-up frequencies run only after convergence. Orbital export,
when requested, runs at the final converged geometry. Nonconvergence is shown
as an error rather than silently accepted. Dummy references are remapped and
references to dummy atoms are rejected before geometry cleanup.
